CHAPTERS OF MY STORY

My Story

Every chapter shaped the person I am becoming.

I
CHILDHOOD

Where It All Began

I grew up as an only child, and perhaps that explains why I was always surrounded by a little extra love—and a lot of extra protection. My parents got married in 1999, and I came into the picture in 2004, after they had to seek medical help just for me to be born.

I was a sickly child and spent quite a lot of time going in and out of hospitals. Because of this, my parents became very protective of me. There were many things I couldn't do, especially activities that might trigger my asthma.

Still, childhood had its own kind of magic. I loved drawing, painting, and playing the piano. I had so many little hobbies and interests that made me happy. Somewhere along the way, though, I slowly stopped doing them. The spark I had for those things faded, and today, those hobbies have become memories of a younger version of me.

II
ELEMENTARY

The “Gifted” Kid

Elementary school was when I became known as the “gifted” kid. I was almost always in the top three of my class, and I joined different competitions representing my school.

I was used to being the student who was expected to do well, and for a while, being good at school felt almost effortless.

But elementary school wasn't all about academics. It was also when I finally got to experience the kind of childhood I had missed because of my asthma. After school, I would play physical games with other kids—running around, laughing, and simply having fun.

Looking back, those ordinary afternoons were some of the moments that made childhood feel like childhood.

III
HIGH SCHOOL

The Quiet Bookworm

High school was where things started to change. I attended our province's national high school, and the version of me that entered those years was very different from the elementary-school girl I used to be.

The energetic and competitive student slowly became the quiet one who was perfectly content sitting in her chair with a book.

Harry Potter Twilight Percy Jackson

This was also when I discovered my love for fictional stories. I started reading novels and became obsessed with worlds beyond my own. I spent my free time reading, and eventually, binge-watching became part of the experience too.

Basically, this was the era when my inner nerd officially came to life.

Then the pandemic happened—and somehow, it made me even more comfortable in my own little world of books, movies, and fictional characters.

IV
COLLEGE

From Nonchalant to Chalant

Today, I am studying at Mindanao State University–Iligan Institute of Technology, one of the most prestigious universities in Mindanao.

College introduced me to a version of myself I didn't expect to meet.

The nonchalant Anthea became the chalant Anthea.

Maybe the pandemic had something to do with it. Maybe growing up did. Or maybe college simply gave me more reasons to care.

This was also where I realized something humbling: I wasn't necessarily as gifted as I once thought I was. In elementary school, I was used to being one of the best in the room. In college, I met people who were far more brilliant, hardworking, and talented than I was.

At first, that realization could have been discouraging. Instead, it became one of the things that pushed me forward.

I learned that I didn't have to be the smartest person in the room. I just had to keep learning, keep improving, and keep showing up.

And yes, being a nursing student makes that easier said than done. Between lectures, requirements, exams, and exhausting clinical duties, there are definitely days when I question every life decision that brought me here.

But somehow, I'm still here.

And I'm learning to enjoy the journey—even when the journey comes with very little sleep.

WHO I AM TODAY

Still Becoming

Today, I don't think of myself as the gifted child, the quiet bookworm, or even just the nursing student anymore. I think I'm a little bit of all of them.

I'm still the child who loves creating, even if I no longer draw or play the piano like I used to. I'm still the student who wants to do well, but I've learned that grades aren't the only measure of what I'm capable of.

I'm still the girl who can disappear into a fictional world for hours, but now I also have a real world that I'm learning to navigate.

Most importantly, I've learned that growing up doesn't always mean becoming a completely different person. Sometimes, it means rediscovering the parts of yourself that you thought you had lost.

Still learning. Still growing. Still figuring things out.

And maybe that's the best part of my story—it's not finished yet.
